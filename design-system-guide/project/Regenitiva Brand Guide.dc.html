Brand Standards · Regenitiva LLC · 2026

The system behind the practice.

This guide is the working reference for anyone building or writing for Regenitiva. Every color, size, and rule here is production-ready. Follow it exactly; the restraint is the point.

Who we are

A regenerative medicine MSO built for physicians who already have a practice.

Regenitiva does not treat patients. We give physiatrists, sports medicine physicians, and pain management physicians a turnkey way to add office-based PRP injection services — spine, cervical, and joint — to the practice they already run. Proprietary PRP, AAAHC-aligned compliance programs, clinical protocols, staff training, credentialing, outcome tracking, and revenue operations. The physician performs medicine. We run the business and compliance apparatus behind it.

02 · Voice & Messaging

The emotion belongs to the reader, not the copy.

Regenitiva is restrained and authoritative — trustworthy, expert, innovative, honest. Economical, certain, never raised. Write calm, declarative, specific sentences and let the implications land on their own. The physician should finish the paragraph excited; the paragraph itself never is.

Sentence length

Short. Average 14 words. One idea per sentence.

Punctuation

Periods. No exclamation points anywhere, in any asset.

Person

“You” is the physician. “We” is Regenitiva. Never “our clients.”

Proof

Name the mechanism instead of the adjective. Protocol, audit, chart, outcome.

The money rule — hard constraint

Improved reimbursement is a core message and it is never the opening. Lead with clinical capability; economics follow as the consequence. Better modality → better outcomes → better reimbursement.

Placement rule: “reimbursement,” “revenue,” “profitable,” and “ROI” may appear in body copy and in a dedicated business-case section. They may never appear in a hero headline, a page title, a meta title, a nav label, or an email subject line.

We write this, not this

We write

Office-based PRP for spine, cervical, and joint indications.

States the modality and the sites. The physician does the math.

Not this

Revolutionary regenerative therapy that is changing medicine.

We write

You add a modality. We add the compliance program behind it.

Division of labor, stated plainly. No promise of ease.

Not this

Unlock a powerful new revenue stream for your practice today.

We write

AAAHC-aligned policies, written for your practice and audited on a schedule.

Names the standard and the cadence. Verifiable.

Not this

Best-in-class, world-class compliance solutions you can trust.

We write

A 45-year-old patient scheduled for lumbar surgery went on to be pain-free without it.

One patient, described accurately. No extrapolation to a rate.

Not this

Patients avoid surgery and walk out pain-free.

We write

Documented indications and standardized protocols make the claim defensible.

Compliance framing. The subject is the chart, not the check.

Not this

Bill more per visit with our proven coding strategy.

We write

We track outcomes because evidence should decide what stays in the protocol.

Open-minded and research-forward without claiming a result.

Not this

Our cutting-edge proprietary science delivers superior results.

We write

You perform the procedure. We handle credentialing, documentation, and audit readiness.

Three concrete nouns beat one abstraction.

Not this

We take care of everything so you can finally relax.

We write

PRP is performed in your office, on your schedule, by your staff — trained and competency-checked by us.

Specific about who does what and where.

Not this

Seamless, effortless integration into any practice, guaranteed.

Preferred vocabulary
innovative continued research open-minded patient-focused evidence protocol defensible standardized outcomes
Banned words
fraud overpriced unnecessary money-focused revolutionary game-changing cutting-edge miracle guaranteed maximize revenue upcode easy money

Also banned: exclamation points, any phrasing that implies billing for care that was not indicated, and any claim of a result rate we have not published.

03 · Logo

A droplet with a circular arrow: platelet-rich plasma, regenerating.

The mark is an open droplet drawn in the amber gradient (#FFCD4D at the tip → #F59621 at the base) with a circular arrow completing the loop inside it — platelet-rich plasma and regeneration in one form. It sits beside the wordmark, set in Roboto SemiBold, in #231F20. The gradient belongs to the mark; the wordmark is always solid.

Default. Header, footer, documents, email signature. Minimum width 140px.

Regenitiva
Stacked variant

Narrow columns, square placements, conference badges. Minimum width 96px.

Mark only

Only where the name already appears within the same view. Minimum 32px.

One-color reversed (bone)

Required on ink-900 and on photography. The gradient mark is never used on dark.

Clear space

Clear space on all four sides is a minimum of 1× the width of the droplet. Nothing enters that field — no type, no rules, no image edges, no other logos.

04 · Color

Amber is an accent, never a field.

Amber should occupy roughly 5–10% of any screen. The other 90% is warm dark neutrals and bone. That ratio is what keeps a bright hue reading as premium and confident rather than loud. Every neutral in this system is warm-biased; there are no cool greys anywhere in the brand.

05 · Typography

Archivo for structure. Roboto for everything you read.

Roboto is the wordmark face and the right choice for body and UI — neutral where neutrality is an asset. It is also the most-used interface font on earth, so headlines use Archivo instead: a grotesque with more structural character that still pairs cleanly with the lockup. No Light weights anywhere in this brand.

Rules
  • Never use Roboto Light, Archivo Light, or any weight below 400. This brand should never look delicate.
  • Headlines are tightly tracked. Body copy is tracked at 0; do not letter-space running text.
  • Body measure caps at 72ch; lead paragraphs at 62ch.
  • Sentence case for headlines, buttons, and nav. Uppercase is reserved for the eyebrow style.
  • Mobile: display drops to 40px, h1 to 34px, h2 to 27px, h3 to 23px. Body stays 17px.

Optional editorial accent — Fraunces

An option, not a default. Fraunces is permitted only in pull quotes and patient outcome stories, where a warmer editorial voice helps. It never appears in navigation, buttons, headings, or body copy. If in doubt, don't load it.

Accordion — FAQ

Who performs the injections? +

You do. Regenitiva does not treat patients. We train and competency-check your staff, supply the protocol, and maintain the documentation the procedure generates.

How is this billed defensibly? +

Documented indications, standardized protocols, and a chart that supports what was done. Our compliance program is written to AAAHC standards and audited on a schedule, so the record stands on its own.

What does the practice have to provide? +

A procedure room, staff time for training, and a physician willing to follow the protocol. Equipment, policies, workflows, and reporting come from us.

How long until the service line is running? +

It depends on credentialing and room readiness. We scope that in the practice review before anything is committed to a date.

08 · Imagery

Two tracks. Keep them separate.

Physician-facing imagery carries most of the site. Patient-outcome imagery appears only in evidence modules, where it functions as proof a physician can show a patient. Never mix the two tracks inside one section.

Track one · Physician-facing

Clinical environments, procedure detail, documentation

Physicians in practice. Rooms that look like real offices, not sets. Warm natural light, shallow depth of field, muted and slightly desaturated so amber accents stay dominant on the page.

Track two · Patient outcome (evidence modules only)

Before-and-after, mobility, ordinary life

Real people in ordinary settings. No glamour lighting, no styled wardrobe, no gym-hero framing. Every use requires written patient consent and the caption-style disclaimer.

Shoot for
  • Warm natural light, single source, soft falloff
  • Shallow depth of field — one subject in focus
  • Real rooms, real staff, real paperwork
  • Muted, slightly desaturated color so amber stays the brightest thing on screen
  • Horizon-level framing; the physician is a peer, not a subject
Never
  • Stock-photo handshakes
  • Rows of smiling models in lab coats
  • Glowing-blue “science” abstractions, DNA helices, particle renders
  • Cool-toned or clinical-white grading
  • Any image with visible PHI, or a patient without written consent

09 · Do's & Don'ts

The short list. If a page breaks one of these, it is off-brand.

Do
  • Keep amber to roughly 5–10% of any screen.
  • Use amber-800 for amber-colored type on light; amber-300 on ink-900.
  • Lead with clinical capability; let economics follow in body copy.
  • Write short declarative sentences with specific nouns.
  • Set headlines in Archivo 600 or 700, tightly tracked.
  • Keep radii between 4 and 8px.
  • Cast shadows in warm ink-900 at low alpha.
  • Attribute every testimonial and disclaim every outcome story.
  • Warm, muted, slightly desaturated photography.
  • Use the reversed bone lockup on dark surfaces and photography.
Don't
  • Don't fill a section or a button with the amber gradient.
  • Don't set amber-500 as text on a light background — 2.27:1, fails.
  • Don't put revenue, ROI, or profit in a headline, page title, or nav label.
  • Don't use exclamation points or hype adjectives, anywhere.
  • Don't imply care that was not indicated, or aggressive billing.
  • Don't introduce a cool grey, a pure black, or any red.
  • Don't use Light weights or set body copy below 15px.
  • Don't round anything into a pill or a bubble.
  • Don't run the gradient mark below 32px — use the simplified icon.
  • Don't publish a statistic Regenitiva cannot source.
