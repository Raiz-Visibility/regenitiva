The Gap Most Practices Don't See Coming

AAAHC alignment isn't a single certificate — it's an ongoing standard your policies, training records, and procedures either meet or don't. Most practices find out they've fallen behind during a review, which is the worst possible time to find out.

This checklist covers the seven places we see gaps most often, in the order we'd fix them if we were starting today.

"We assumed our policies were current because we'd never had a problem. We hadn't looked at them in three years." — Practice administrator, multi-site group

The Seven-Point Checklist

Work through these in order. Each one is quick to check and expensive to have missed.

1
Policies reflect current practice

Written procedures should match what actually happens in the room, not what happened when they were drafted.

2
Competency records are current

Every staff member with hands in the process needs a dated, signed record — not a one-time onboarding note.

3
Internal audits are actually scheduled

A quality management plan that exists on paper but never runs is functionally the same as not having one.

4
Documentation ties back to protocol

Chart notes should reference the specific protocol followed, not a general description of the procedure.

What This Looks Like in Practice

—A binder that's reviewed quarterly, not filed once and forgotten
—Sign-off sheets with actual dates, not a single blanket signature
—An audit calendar someone owns, with findings tracked to resolution
Why this matters

Reviewers aren't looking for perfection — they're looking for a system that's actually running. A practice with modest documentation that's clearly maintained will do better than one with an elaborate binder no one has touched in a year.

Getting Started

If you're not sure where your program stands today, the fastest path is an outside review. Read more about our full approach on the Compliance & Operations page, or see how this fits into a complete build on the practice development service page.

Takeaways

Alignment is maintained, not achieved once. Build the quarterly habit and the rest of this list takes care of itself.
