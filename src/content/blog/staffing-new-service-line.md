## You Probably Don't Need to Hire

The most common question practices ask before launching a regenerative medicine service line is whether they need to hire new staff. In most cases, the answer is no — the existing team can absorb the new roles with training and workflow adjustment.

## How the Roles Split

A typical launch involves three existing roles taking on additional responsibilities:

- **Medical assistants** handle preparation and room setup, trained to competency on the specific protocol
- **Front desk staff** manage the scheduling, consent, and documentation workflow
- **The physician** performs the procedure within the standardized protocol

The key is that each role has a clearly defined scope with documented training, not an informal understanding of who does what.

## Training Is the Bottleneck, Not Headcount

The constraint isn't the number of people — it's the time required to train them to competency and document that training. A practice that tries to launch before training is complete ends up with inconsistent execution and gaps in the documentation.

## When You Do Need to Hire

The exception is high-volume practices or multi-site groups where the procedure volume exceeds what existing staff can absorb alongside their current responsibilities. Even then, the new hire is typically one additional medical assistant, not a new department.

## Takeaways

Start with the team you have, train them properly, and document the training. Hiring follows volume, not launch.
