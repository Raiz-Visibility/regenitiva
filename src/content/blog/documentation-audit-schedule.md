## Why Most Audit Schedules Fail

The most common failure mode isn't a bad schedule — it's a schedule no one follows. Practices create an ambitious internal audit plan, run it once, and then let it lapse until an external review forces them back to the table.

A schedule that works is one that's simple enough to maintain quarter after quarter without heroic effort.

## A Quarterly Schedule That Works

Each quarter, dedicate half a day to three tasks:

- **Chart review** — pull five random charts from the quarter and check each one against the protocol. Are the notes specific? Do they reference the protocol followed? Is the documentation complete?
- **Training record check** — verify that every staff member involved in procedures has a current, dated competency record. Flag anyone whose last sign-off is more than twelve months old.
- **Policy review** — confirm that written policies still match current practice. If a workflow has changed, update the policy and date the revision.

## Documenting Findings

The audit isn't complete until the findings are written down and assigned to someone. A finding without an owner and a deadline is just a note.

- Document what was reviewed and what was found
- Assign each finding to a specific person with a resolution date
- Review the previous quarter's findings to verify they were resolved

## Takeaways

A simple quarterly audit that actually runs is worth more than an elaborate annual plan that doesn't. The goal is a habit, not a project.
