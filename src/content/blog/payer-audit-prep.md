## Why the Letter Shouldn't Be the Starting Gun

A payer audit notification triggers panic in practices that have let their documentation drift. But for practices that treat compliance as an ongoing system, the notification is just a calendar event.

The difference isn't the quality of the medicine — it's whether the records supporting that medicine have been maintained in real time or reconstructed after the fact.

## What Auditors Actually Look For

Auditors aren't looking for perfection. They're looking for a system that's running — documentation that's current, training records that are dated, and policies that match what's happening in the room.

- Chart notes that reference specific protocols, not general descriptions
- Competency records with dates, not blanket sign-offs
- An internal audit schedule with documented findings and resolutions
- Policies reviewed on a published schedule, not just when someone asks

## The Quarterly Habit

The single most effective audit preparation is a quarterly internal review. Pull a sample of charts, check them against the protocol, verify training records are current, and document what you found.

This takes half a day per quarter. Reconstructing the same information after an audit notification takes weeks and produces visibly rushed documentation.

## Takeaways

Audit readiness is a byproduct of ongoing compliance work. If your documentation is current today, it will be current when the letter arrives.
