## Three Variables, Not One

PRP preparation isn't a single process — it's a set of decisions about concentration, cell count, and activation method. Each one affects outcomes independently, and most practices are only controlling for one of the three.

## Concentration

The volume of platelet-rich plasma relative to the total preparation matters more than most protocols acknowledge. A preparation that's too dilute delivers fewer growth factors; one that's too concentrated may not distribute evenly at the injection site.

- Standardize the preparation volume for each indication
- Document the concentration achieved, not just the process followed
- Track outcomes against concentration data to identify the effective range

## Cell Count

Platelet count in the final preparation varies by patient, by draw volume, and by centrifuge settings. A protocol that doesn't account for this variation is producing inconsistent treatments.

## Activation Method

Whether and how the preparation is activated before delivery affects the release kinetics of growth factors at the site. Different indications may benefit from different activation approaches.

## Why This Matters for Documentation

When each variable is documented per procedure, outcome tracking becomes meaningful. Without it, you're tracking results against an unknown input.

## Takeaways

Control for all three variables, document each one per procedure, and track outcomes against the full set. A protocol that specifies only the process but not the parameters isn't a protocol — it's a suggestion.
