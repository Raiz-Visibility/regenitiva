## Why Liens Get Disputed

The most common reason a lien is disputed isn't the treatment itself — it's the documentation behind it. A chart that doesn't clearly support the medical necessity of each procedure gives the opposing side an opening.

## What Strong Lien Documentation Looks Like

Every treatment in a lien case needs to tie back to a documented indication, a standardized protocol, and a chart note that connects the two.

- Document the specific indication and the clinical reasoning
- Reference the protocol followed for each procedure
- Maintain competency records for every staff member involved

## The Compliance Connection

Lien documentation and compliance documentation overlap almost entirely. A practice with strong AAAHC-aligned policies already has most of what it needs for defensible lien records.

## Takeaways

The documentation that makes a lien defensible is the same documentation that makes your program audit-ready. Build one system, not two.
