## One Protocol Does Not Fit All

Spine, cervical, and joint conditions present differently and respond differently to treatment. A practice running a single preparation and delivery method across all indications is leaving outcomes on the table.

## How Indication-Specific Protocols Work

Each indication defines the preparation parameters, the delivery approach, and the documentation that goes with it. This isn't about having more options — it's about matching the treatment to the problem.

- Spine indications require different preparation concentrations than peripheral joints
- Cervical approaches have distinct positioning and delivery considerations
- Joint protocols vary by the specific structure being treated

## The Documentation Difference

When each procedure references a specific protocol, the chart tells a clearer story. This matters for outcomes tracking and for any future review of the documentation.

## Takeaways

Matching preparation and delivery to the specific indication improves outcomes and produces cleaner documentation. A uniform protocol is simpler to run but harder to defend.
