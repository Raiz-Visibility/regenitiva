## A Different Revenue Cycle

Regenerative medicine billing involves considerations that standard office visits don't — lien management for motor vehicle accident patients, documentation requirements that go beyond the chart note, and payer-specific rules that vary by state.

Practices that treat the revenue cycle as an afterthought find out the hard way that undocumented procedures are unchargeable procedures.

## Documentation Drives Revenue

The connection between clinical documentation and revenue is direct. A procedure performed under a standardized protocol with complete chart notes is defensible. A procedure documented with a general note and no protocol reference is a dispute waiting to happen.

- Each procedure should reference the specific protocol followed
- The indication and clinical reasoning should be documented before the procedure, not after
- Competency records for the staff involved should be current and accessible

## Lien-Specific Considerations

For practices treating motor vehicle accident patients, the lien is only as strong as the documentation behind it. A well-documented lien with clear medical necessity, standardized protocols, and complete records will hold up. A poorly documented one won't.

## The Role of Compliance

Revenue cycle management and compliance aren't separate functions — they're the same function viewed from different angles. The documentation that makes a procedure billable is the same documentation that makes it audit-ready.

## Takeaways

Build the documentation system first and the revenue follows. Trying to optimize billing without a compliance foundation produces short-term gains and long-term risk.
