## It's a Conversation, Not a Pitch

The practices that introduce regenerative medicine most effectively don't lead with the treatment — they lead with the problem the patient already came in with. The treatment is one option among several, presented in the context of what the patient has already tried.

## Framing the Discussion

Patients who are running out of conservative options but aren't ready for surgery are the most receptive audience. They're already looking for something between what they've tried and what they've been told is next.

- Start with what the patient has already done and how it's working
- Present regenerative medicine as one option alongside others
- Be specific about what it involves — preparation, procedure, recovery timeline
- Be honest about what the evidence supports and what it doesn't

## What Patients Actually Want to Know

Most patients have three questions: does it work, what does it cost, and how long until they know. Answer all three directly, with specifics about your own outcomes data where available.

> "The patients who do best are the ones who understand exactly what we're doing and why. The conversation is the treatment." — Physiatrist, California practice

## Documentation

Document the discussion in the chart — what was presented, what the patient asked, and what they decided. This protects the practice and creates a record that supports the clinical reasoning.

## Takeaways

Present regenerative medicine as a clinical option, not a product. The conversation should sound like medicine, not marketing.
